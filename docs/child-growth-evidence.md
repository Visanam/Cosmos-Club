# Child-Growth Outcome Framework — Research Notes

The values-first content model is grounded in social and emotional learning as a practical set of skills that children can notice, rehearse, and apply in everyday settings. The website intentionally frames each value as a **real-life moment**, a **child practice**, and a **growth outcome**, rather than as a diagnosis or promised result.

CASEL describes social and emotional learning as developing healthy identities, managing emotions and goals, showing empathy, maintaining supportive relationships, and making caring decisions. Its five interconnected competencies are self-awareness, self-management, social awareness, relationship skills, and responsible decision-making. The framework notes that these skills can be practised across childhood and in home, school, and community contexts. [CASEL, “What Is SEL?”](https://casel.org/what-is-sel/)

Harvard Graduate School of Education’s SECURe project reports that children’s academic and social-emotional functioning can be improved by targeting executive functioning and emotional and behavioural regulation through curriculum. It also stresses that sustained skill development depends on continued access to supportive conditions and practices. [Harvard EASEL Lab, “SECURe”](https://easel.gse.harvard.edu/secure)

Lawson et al. identify recurring components in evidence-based elementary social-emotional programs, including social skills, identifying others’ feelings, identifying one’s own feelings, and behavioural coping or relaxation skills. Their analysis supports a modular approach that can be tailored to needs and context. [Lawson et al., 2019, *Prevention Science*](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC6544145/)
